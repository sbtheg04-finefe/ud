# PlatePair Creator Business Hub Refactor Spec

This document instructs Replit to examine the current PlatePair/FineFeud rendition and refactor it toward a creator-first business platform where competition is the attraction layer and monetization lives inside each creator’s dashboard.[cite:4][cite:62][cite:109]

The target product is no longer just a battle app and no longer a disguised booking or store clone. The target product is a food creator hub where a creator, seller, or coach can host challenges, sell digital products, offer 1-on-1 sessions, and use competition as the engine that brings attention and repeat visits.[cite:62][cite:63][cite:111][cite:115]

## Core product shift

Replit should preserve the best of the current app, especially the existing battle, arena, discovery, and participation logic, but reinterpret the product around this rule: **every creator should have a monetizable home base, and every challenge should feed that home base**.[cite:112][cite:115]

The old mental model was close to:
- recipe battle app,
- social challenge app,
- or link-to-arena combat engine.[cite:4][cite:109][cite:112]

The new mental model must become:
- creator business hub for food creators and sellers,
- challenge engine as audience acquisition,
- product/session sales as monetization,
- profile/storefront as the destination.[cite:62][cite:63][cite:111]

Replit should treat this as a product architecture change, not a cosmetic UI refresh.[cite:115]

## Product thesis

PlatePair should let a food creator or food seller do all of the following from one account:

- Host public or private food competitions.
- Allow users to claim a spot in a competition instead of merely “booking.”
- Sell digital products such as recipe packs, ebooks, meal plans, or downloadable guides.
- Offer 1-on-1 sessions such as coaching, consultation, cook-alongs, or personalized learning.
- Optionally surface physical offerings such as weekly drops, trays, tastings, or preorder-style offers later.
- Turn social attention into recurring actions: join, vote, buy, book, follow, return.[cite:62][cite:63][cite:64][cite:111][cite:115]

This direction aligns with broader creator monetization and social commerce trends where creators increasingly need stacked revenue streams, and where commerce works best when embedded into interactive audience experiences.[cite:68][cite:120][cite:123][cite:126][cite:129][cite:141]

## North-star positioning

The product should feel like:

> A food creator platform where your audience can compete, buy, and book.

Alternative internal framing:

> Turn any recipe, dish, or food idea into a joinable competition, then convert that attention into ebook sales, session bookings, and creator-led monetization.[cite:4][cite:62][cite:111]

This is stronger than trying to make a booking plugin act like ecommerce, and stronger than making PlatePair only a pure competition destination.[page:2][cite:115]

## Who the first users are

Replit should optimize the current app for these first user types, in this order:

1. Small food creators on Instagram/Facebook/TikTok.
2. Home bakers and dessert sellers.
3. Meal-prep creators or local plate sellers.
4. Cooking coaches and food educators.
5. Local chefs using social content to build an audience.[web:72][web:74][web:75][cite:62]

These users often do not need a huge ecommerce backend first. They need a way to attract attention, run simple events, sell a few high-intent offers, and look organized.[web:72][web:75][web:136]

## Primary UX rule

The app must stop feeling like a generic community feed with battles attached. It must start feeling like a set of creator-owned mini businesses powered by challenge mechanics.[cite:115][cite:112]

Every major creator-facing screen should answer one of these:
- How do I attract attention?
- How do I convert that attention?
- How do I manage who joined, bought, or booked?
- How do I make my profile worth revisiting?

Every participant-facing screen should answer one of these:
- What challenge can I join?
- What is this creator selling?
- Can I book time with them?
- Why should I return after this battle ends?

## Refactor priority

Replit should not rebuild the whole app from zero. It should inspect current working systems and selectively reframe them.

Keep if already usable:
- battle creation,
- arena objects,
- upload/submission flows,
- judging and voting logic,
- leaderboards,
- user profiles,
- share mechanics,
- anything already stable around link ingestion and challenge lifecycles.[cite:109][cite:112][cite:115]

Refactor heavily:
- dashboard IA (information architecture),
- creator profile meaning,
- monetization surfaces,
- CTA hierarchy,
- onboarding flow,
- navigation labels,
- object model naming where needed.

Add net-new carefully:
- creator storefront,
- digital product management,
- session/booking management,
- creator earnings/leads/orders views,
- challenge-to-product conversion surfaces.

## Core platform objects

Replit should structure the app around these core objects:

### 1. Creator
A creator is not just a user. A creator has:
- creator profile,
- public storefront,
- challenge hosting capability,
- product catalog,
- session offerings,
- audience list,
- creator analytics.

### 2. Challenge
A challenge is the main acquisition and engagement object.
It has:
- title,
- host creator,
- theme/source link,
- public/private mode,
- entry cap,
- registration start/end,
- submission deadline,
- voting window,
- prize/reward,
- paid/free entry toggle,
- featured products beneath it,
- optional session upsell.

### 3. Product
A product is a creator-owned digital or simple offer.
Types for v1:
- ebook,
- recipe pack,
- guide,
- meal plan PDF,
- template/download.

### 4. Session
A session is a creator-offered time-based service.
Types for v1:
- 1-on-1 coaching,
- consultation,
- virtual cook-along,
- personalized review.

### 5. Submission
Participant entry into a challenge.
Should connect to creator, challenge, user, media, optional notes, and score/judging status.[cite:66][cite:115]

### 6. Audience action
A unified event model for:
- joined challenge,
- submitted entry,
- voted,
- purchased product,
- booked session,
- followed creator,
- shared challenge.

This event model should feed analytics and creator dashboards.

## Navigation refactor

Replit should redesign top-level navigation around the new model.

### Participant/global nav
Recommended tabs:
- Discover
- Challenges
- Creators
- Saved
- Profile

### Creator dashboard nav
Recommended tabs:
- Overview
- Challenges
- Products
- Sessions
- Audience
- Settings

If current app structure already has Arena, Battles, Hacks, or similar tabs, preserve what works but rename or reframe where needed so it feels creator-economy native rather than game-only.[cite:108][cite:115]

## Public creator page

Every creator should have a public page that works like a lightweight storefront plus challenge hub.

Sections:
- Hero with creator name, bio, niche, credibility markers.
- Current live challenge(s).
- Top digital products.
- Book a session.
- Past winners or highlighted submissions.
- Social proof or metrics.
- Follow/share actions.

The creator page should feel like the place challenge traffic lands and then converts.[cite:63][cite:64][cite:111]

## Challenge entry refactor

The key conceptual change:

Do not present the main CTA as “book” unless needed for sessions.
Present it as:
- Join challenge
- Claim your spot
- Enter this battle
- Reserve your entry

This preserves scarcity and urgency without forcing the app into appointment language.[cite:62][cite:109][cite:112]

Challenge entry flow for participants:
1. View challenge details.
2. See creator profile snapshot and reward.
3. Tap Join / Claim Spot.
4. Pay entry fee if required.
5. Confirm submission requirements and deadlines.
6. Upload before deadline.
7. Vote or invite others after entry.
8. See creator offers below or after joining.

## Creator dashboard: Overview tab

This should become the creator’s command center.

Required cards/widgets:
- Live challenges count.
- Entries this week.
- Product sales count.
- Session bookings count.
- Recent audience actions.
- Top converting challenge.
- Quick actions: create challenge, add product, add session, share profile.

The overview should answer: “Is PlatePair helping this creator grow and monetize?”[web:129][web:136][web:141]

## Creator dashboard: Challenges tab

Features:
- Create challenge.
- Edit challenge.
- Set title/theme/source link.
- Set private/public.
- Set entry fee or free entry.
- Cap seats.
- Set registration/submission/voting windows.
- Add prize text.
- Attach featured product and optional session upsell.
- See participant list and submission status.
- View share link and referral metrics.

This should build on existing battle creation flows, not discard them.[cite:109][cite:112][cite:115]

## Creator dashboard: Products tab

V1 should support simple digital product sales.

Needed features:
- Product title.
- Cover image.
- Short and long description.
- Price.
- Product file or access link.
- Status: draft/published.
- Purchase count.
- Revenue count.
- Attach to creator profile.
- Attach to challenge pages as featured upsell.

Product types for v1:
- recipe ebook,
- meal plan PDF,
- kitchen guide,
- “how I made this” breakdown,
- pantry checklist bundle.

Avoid building a full enterprise ecommerce catalog in v1. Keep it intentionally narrow and creator-friendly.[web:130][web:135][web:140][web:141]

## Creator dashboard: Sessions tab

V1 should support 1-on-1 learning or consultation offers.

Needed fields:
- session title,
- description,
- duration,
- price,
- availability basics,
- booking CTA,
- confirmation message,
- session type (video, phone, in-person, virtual cook-along).

If the current build has no booking engine, Replit can implement a simple lightweight booking request flow first, then evolve later.[page:2][web:90]

The session feature is important because creators often monetize via coaching and personalized help in addition to digital downloads.[web:129][web:132][web:141]

## Creator dashboard: Audience tab

This should show the creator the people PlatePair is generating.

Minimum sections:
- New followers.
- Challenge entrants.
- Product buyers.
- Session bookings.
- Most engaged users.
- Export/contact notes later if applicable.

The product becomes much more valuable when creators can see that competitions are not random fun but measurable audience generation.[cite:63][cite:64][web:129]

## Challenge page monetization surfaces

Each challenge page should be designed as both an event and a conversion page.

Below the main challenge panel, include:
- Host creator card.
- Featured digital product.
- Book a session CTA.
- “More from this creator.”
- Past challenge results or proof.

This is essential. Replit must stop treating battle pages as isolated gameplay objects and start treating them as monetizable creator funnels.[cite:62][cite:63][cite:111]

## Discovery page changes

Discovery should not be only trending battles. It should blend:
- trending challenges,
- featured creators,
- top-selling creator products,
- upcoming sessions,
- recent winning submissions,
- category filters like baking, meal prep, Caribbean, desserts, healthy meals, etc.[cite:65][cite:108]

This creates a healthier ecosystem where creators can be discovered for both competition and commerce.[cite:115]

## Onboarding split

Current onboarding should be refactored so users choose their mode early.

Modes:
- Join and compete.
- Create and host.
- Sell and teach.

The “creator” path should unlock dashboard setup.
The “participant” path should stay light and fast.

This reduces confusion and aligns the app with actual user intent.[cite:115][cite:110]

## Conversion loops

Replit should wire the following loops clearly.

### Loop A: creator growth loop
1. Creator creates challenge.
2. Shares link on social.
3. Audience joins challenge.
4. Participants create submissions and shares.
5. New users discover creator.
6. Some buy product or book session.
7. Creator sees outcome and hosts again.[cite:63][cite:64][cite:111]

### Loop B: participant-to-customer loop
1. User joins challenge.
2. User enjoys creator’s content and battle format.
3. User sees ebook/guide/session offer.
4. User buys or books.
5. User follows creator and returns.[cite:62][cite:63]

### Loop C: battle-to-storefront loop
1. Challenge attracts attention.
2. Challenge page routes users to creator page.
3. Creator page shows products and sessions.
4. Creator page becomes monetization destination.[cite:111][cite:112]

## Data and analytics requirements

Replit should ensure the backend can track:
- challenge views,
- joins,
- completed submissions,
- votes,
- shares,
- product clicks,
- product purchases,
- session clicks,
- session bookings,
- creator profile visits.

This data is necessary because creators will not stick around if they cannot see business value.[web:129][web:141]

## UI tone and design direction

The app should feel more like a polished creator commerce platform with competitive energy than a generic game or generic marketplace.[web:136][web:129]

Visual principles:
- creator identity should be visible,
- challenge urgency should be clear,
- monetization blocks should feel integrated,
- public pages should look shareable and credible,
- dashboard should feel operational and useful.

Avoid making the dashboard feel like pure admin software. It should still carry the excitement of food culture, competition, and audience energy.[cite:113][cite:115]

## Specific screens Replit should inspect and alter

Replit should audit the current rendition and modify these categories of screens if they exist:

- Home/discover screen.
- Arena/challenge detail page.
- Battle creation flow.
- User profile page.
- Dashboard or account area.
- Hacks/content ingestion area.
- Submission upload flow.
- Leaderboard and result pages.[cite:108][cite:109][cite:112][cite:115]

For each one, ask:
- does this support creator monetization,
- does this route users toward a creator-owned destination,
- does this clarify the creator/participant distinction,
- does this create a path from fun to paid value?

## MVP implementation order

Replit should prioritize in this sequence:

### Phase 1: architecture and IA
- Add creator dashboard navigation.
- Reframe profiles into creator pages.
- Add creator role/mode.
- Add challenge-to-creator funnel surfaces.

### Phase 2: monetization primitives
- Digital products model and simple product management.
- Session model and simple session booking/request flow.
- Attach products/sessions to creator pages and challenge pages.

### Phase 3: conversion and analytics
- Event tracking for joins, sales, and bookings.
- Dashboard overview metrics.
- Better CTA placement.

### Phase 4: polish
- stronger onboarding split,
- richer creator page,
- better discovery blending challenges plus creators plus offers,
- social sharing/referral reinforcement.[cite:63][cite:64][cite:111]

## Important anti-goals

Replit should avoid these mistakes:

- Do not turn PlatePair into a generic ecommerce clone.
- Do not turn PlatePair into a generic booking platform.
- Do not bury the competition engine; it is the hook.
- Do not keep battle pages isolated from creator monetization.
- Do not make the creator dashboard overly enterprise or bloated.
- Do not force too many product types in v1.

The app wins by combining challenge energy with creator-owned monetization, not by replacing Shopify, Gumroad, or Calendly in full.[web:129][web:135][web:136]

## Functional acceptance criteria

The refactor is successful only if the following are true:

1. A creator can host a challenge from their dashboard.
2. A creator can publish at least one digital product.
3. A creator can publish at least one 1-on-1 session offer.
4. A public creator page shows challenges, products, and sessions together.
5. A participant can join a challenge and then discover the creator’s monetization offers.
6. The dashboard overview shows evidence of audience and monetization activity.
7. The current battle logic still feels strong, but now feeds a creator-owned business destination.

## Replit instruction style

Replit should examine current components, routes, schema, and flows before rewriting. Preserve stable logic where possible. Prefer incremental migration over full rebuild. When naming conflicts exist between “battle,” “arena,” “challenge,” and “creator,” choose the language that best supports this new creator-business direction while keeping user understanding high.[cite:109][cite:115]

## Final instruction to Replit

Treat PlatePair as evolving from a battle app into a **creator commerce and challenge platform for food creators**. The challenge engine remains the emotional hook, but the creator dashboard, creator storefront, digital products, and 1-on-1 sessions become the monetization spine.[cite:62][cite:111][web:129][web:136]

The goal is not merely more features. The goal is a clearer product truth:

> creators use PlatePair to turn audience attention into participation, and participation into products, bookings, and repeat business.[cite:63][cite:64][web:141]
